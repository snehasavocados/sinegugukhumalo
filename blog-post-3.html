Building "Designed to Learn": Notes on My Personal Brand

If you work in Information Systems and Technology, it's naive to think you can avoid having a presence online. Employers already look. Lecturers already look. The question was never whether I'd have a digital footprint — it was whether I'd shape it on purpose, or leave it to whatever a random collection of posts and tags happened to say about me.

So I built this site: Designed to Learn. The name is meant literally two ways. It's about learning — my research, my teaching, the things I'm figuring out. And it's designed, deliberately, rather than defaulted into.

The unglamorous part came first: getting a domain, sorting out hosting, and untangling a DNS misconfiguration that left the site returning a cPanel 404 for longer than I'd like to admit. It's a useful reminder that a personal brand isn't just copywriting — it sits on infrastructure, and infrastructure breaks in boring, fixable ways if you're willing to debug it.

The more interesting part was deciding what actually belongs here. Not just a polished, professional-only version of myself, but something that's actually representative: my research interests, my teaching, the roles I hold, and enough of my own voice that it reads like a person rather than a CV with a template around it. I searched my own name before I built anything, honestly — checked what a stranger, or a future employer, would already find. That audit shaped what I chose to add here as much as anything else did.

This site will keep changing. That's the point. A personal brand isn't a document you finish — it's closer to the "now" panel on my homepage: current, and worth revisiting.
